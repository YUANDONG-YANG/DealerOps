Progress against the sprint plan

We entered the week behind on the extension scope and ended it with that scope delivered, so the week closed ahead of where the Sprint 1 plan had it. Both milestones landed: the instructor demonstration on 21 October and Status Report 2 with its deck on 23 October. The work below is grouped by outcome rather than by commit.

1

Closed the requirement gaps carried from week 1.

Several items previously marked "won't do" were reopened and delivered — the ad rules, account handling and the DMS/CRM pages (8f18e8c, 02f6ecc), followed by rate limiting, one table per view and customer retention (34f5db2). Each item was signed off against the requirement ledger, so the reopened scope stays traceable.

2

Ran a manual bug hunt and cleared it the same day.

A hand-run browser CRUD walkthrough on 20 October surfaced ten defects the automated suite had passed. All ten were fixed and re-verified that day (c872753), with findings logged in review/DealerOps-Browser-CRUD-Bug-Log-2026-10-20.md. I want to keep this check: it caught things a green test run did not.

3

Shipped the bulk of the extension scope.

21 October was the heavy build day — the buyer site, payments, bill of sale, dashboard and workshop reports (c0cd793), the dealership manager and staff roles (113b440), and audit logs, the buyer-inquiry inbox and buyer-site ad links (e1ca446). The next day added type-ahead search, a disclaimer drawer and explicit empty states (6a5ee3b) plus buyer-site pages named by dealership and ad-compliance filters. One design call stands out: no business data is ever hard-deleted — photos, payments and vehicle links are soft-deleted and the retention job was dropped (8181aa5).

4

Made deployment a single self-verifying command.

Deploy now publishes the local database to Azure with deploy.sh --seed and confirms the import through the gateway before it reports success (d1451c2, f045bd6). It also refuses to run Terraform from a dirty or stale checkout, which closes off a class of "works on my machine" failures.

5

Tightened the UI for the demo.

One link style and theme across the app (cf09126), real values in place of dash placeholders, and themed icons instead of emoji. The demo seed was narrowed to licensed Wikimedia photos (09c20fa) — a licensing decision, not a cosmetic one, and worth noting before we show the product externally.

6

Produced the client and course deliverables.

The client system design briefing page was created and brought up to date (aa308a6, 9298d33), and Status Report 2 and the deck were added with the 21 October demonstration recorded in them (edf99a1, 515d36a). The CPRG-305 Unit 6 bug-hunt submission and the rebaselined Assignment 2 test plan belong to the course track, not the product.

What was delivered

  • The full extension scope in one week: buyer site, payments, bill of sale, dashboard, workshop reporting, audit logs, inquiry inbox and the manager/staff role split.
  • Ten manually found defects fixed and re-verified the same day.
  • A deployment that refuses to run from a dirty checkout and verifies its own import.
  • Status Report 2, the deck, and a demonstration given to the instructor on 21 October.

Impediments and how they were handled

The end-to-end suite drifted twice

As the UI changed under it mid-week, I realigned Playwright with the current screens rather than let the suite rot (34f5db2, 28af891), accepting the rework as the cost of extending UI and tests in the same sprint.

Kudu refused first-connection uploads

This made deployment look broken. Adding a retry on the zip upload turned an intermittent failure into a non-issue (480ae38).

Evidence cut-off was 23 October while the sprint runs to 1 November

The final week is reported as planned work and will be confirmed in the next report — flagged here so the timeline reads honestly.

What went well

Hand-run walkthrough caught real defects

The manual walkthrough caught ten real defects a passing test suite had missed, and it is now a standing step before we accept a change.

One retention policy, decided once

Settling soft delete once and applying it across photos, payments and links together avoided the table-by-table drift we would otherwise have cleaned up later.
